Scope & purpose · v1

PH Referral Gateway

A translation middleware. Our referral and telemedicine systems send plain JSON; the gateway turns it into PH Core and PH eReferral FHIR and delivers it to a destination the sender never has to know about.

Updated 7 Sep 2026 Phase 2 complete First slice shipped FHIR R4 · 4.0.1
01 · Purpose

What this gateway is for

Two of our systems need to send patient referrals and telemedicine requests to other facilities, and eventually to HCPNs across the country. Those destinations expect FHIR conforming to the Philippine IGs. Our systems do not speak FHIR, and should not have to.

The gateway sits between them and does three jobs: it translates our JSON into conformant FHIR, it routes to a destination chosen from a registry it owns, and it resolves patient identity at the receiving end using national IDs.

It is a facade, not a repository. The gateway stores no clinical or demographic data. Every request is translated in flight from a native record that stays where it is. A retry re-reads the source rather than replaying a stored payload. This is the founding constraint. A design that needs the gateway to hold patient data has stopped being a facade, and at that point we would be better off adopting a FHIR server.
02 · Context

The pieces and who owns them

The registry is the reason senders stay simple. They pick an opaque destination id from a list the gateway publishes; endpoints, credentials and transport never leave the gateway. Onboarding a new facility changes nothing in the referral or telemedicine code.

04 · Process

Finding the patient

Identity is solved at capture, not at integration. Policy requires triage to record both PhilSys and PhilHealth for every patient, so the gateway never has to guess whether two people are the same person. Matching on names and birth dates was considered and rejected: its false-match rate is worse than the problem it solves.

Awaiting your confirmation. The red branch is my recommendation, not a locked decision. If PhilSys resolves to one patient and PhilHealth to a different one, the gateway refuses rather than picking. Merging two charts silently combines two people's allergies and medications, and downstream copies make it hard to undo. A refusal is only inconvenient — and each one is a free duplicate report for the MPI.

06 · Decisions

Locked

These survived the review and should not be reopened without a reason that is written down.

AreaDecisionWhy
Scope No external HCPN receives anything in v1 The first receiver is our own second system. Removes an external dependency from the critical path and makes the first slice testable end to end.
Storage No clinical or demographic persistence A facade that needs its own patient store is not a facade. Only operational tables: identity map, referral state, outbox, audit.
Intake Gateway defines its own non-FHIR JSON contract Native systems should never learn FHIR. If a field in the contract looks like FHIR, that is a leak.
Routing Sender picks an opaque hcpn_id, never an endpoint Adding a destination becomes a gateway config change instead of a release of two other systems.
Registry PH Core Organization + R4 Endpoint, hand-seeded from the government facility list No national endpoint directory exists yet. Modelling it as FHIR now means a future directory replaces the seeding, not the design. PH Core already profiles NHFR, HCPN, PAN and PEN identifiers and partOf for hierarchy.
Identity capture Both PhilSys and PhilHealth required at triage, by policy Fixes the problem where it originates. The patient supplies both IDs, so a policy at the desk beats probabilistic matching downstream.
Identity match Either ID is sufficient; neither matches means register The second ID is the fallback for records created before the policy, which may carry only one.
ID propagation Backfill a missing identifier onto a matched record Identifiers converge as traffic flows, so the gap closes itself instead of needing a migration.
Conformance HL7 validator plus pinned IG tarballs, enforced in CI The PHP layer silently drops fields it does not recognise. Without this gate nothing verifies that what we emit is actually conformant.
Access Every read gets a server-side scope filter the caller cannot remove Incoming queries are never trusted. Audit logging is a legal obligation once we broker patient data.
07 · Boundaries

In and out for v1

In

  • Intake API and its JSON contract
  • Destination registry and picklist
  • Translation to PH Core and eReferral
  • Patient lookup, register, ID backfill
  • Referral and telemedicine request types
  • Delivery between our two systems
  • CI conformance gate

Out

  • Delivery to external HCPNs
  • HCPN reading or searching our data
  • Storing any clinical or demographic record
  • Demographic or fuzzy patient matching
  • Attachment hosting — references only
  • Cancelling or amending a sent referral
  • PSGC lookup from address text

Out does not mean never. HCPN read access and outbound delivery stay on the roadmap; they are simply not in the first release, and nothing in the design blocks them.

08 · Risks

Still open, worst first

01 Conflicting identifier hit Built as the safe default: the gateway refuses with a 409 and sends nothing. Still needs your sign-off — reversing it to "pick one" is a config change, but it would merge two charts.
02 Telemedicine does not fit ServiceRequest cleanly Confirmed by the validator. The IG slices occurrence[x] closed on occurrenceDateTime, so a consult window cannot be expressed structurally. We send the start and put the full window in a note. Appointment is the alternative, but the IG does not profile it.
03 MPI is untested and its runtime role is undecided Is it queried per request, or does it only keep the systems clean in the background? The answer changes whether it sits on the critical path.
04 Native systems may not store PSGC codes PH Core address extensions need codes, not place names. If the systems hold only text, a lookup table becomes unscoped work.
05 Attachment transport unknown The contract assumes a fetchable URL. If the systems only have files on disk, the contract changes.
06 Newborns and patients without PhilSys Deliberately left permissive. The registration process is not known yet, so the rule is not written.
07 PSOC code system conflict between the two IGs An upstream defect. We pick one canonical deliberately and record the choice.
08 No verb for withdrawal A sender cannot currently cancel or amend a referral already delivered.
09 · Status

What runs today

The translation layer and the outbound flow are built and green. Three endpoints, two console commands, and four golden fixtures that CI validates against the pinned IGs on every push.

SurfaceWhat it doesState
GET /api/registry/v1/hcpn The destination picklist. Hides facilities with no endpoint; never returns endpoints or credentials. Built
POST /api/intake/v1/requests Plain JSON in, PH eReferral transaction Bundle out, delivered to the receiver. Resolves the patient first, refuses on conflict. Built
GET /api/intake/v1/requests/{id} Delivery status for one submission. Built
fhir:build-referral Translate an intake file without sending it. Regenerates the golden fixtures. Built
fhir:validate Check any resource against the pinned IGs. Built
Per-system auth Bearer tokens on the intake and registry routes. Not wired
These routes have no authentication yet. Do not expose them outside the local network until per-system tokens are in place. It is the first task of the policy phase.

What the validator caught that PHP did not

Each of these was serialised without complaint by the PHP layer and rejected by the HL7 validator. This is the concrete argument for the CI gate being non-optional.

  1. PSGC address extensions need Coding, not CodeableConcept.
  2. A LOINC-coded vital pulls in the base FHIR vital-signs profile, which requires a UCUM system and code on the Quantity — a bare unit string fails.
  3. Provenance.target is restricted to ServiceRequest; targeting the Task too is an error.
  4. ServiceRequest.occurrence[x] is sliced closed on occurrenceDateTime, which is what makes a telemedicine window inexpressible.

Seventy tests, static analysis at level 7, and four fixtures at 0 errors against both IGs.

10 · Sequencing

First slice — built

One referral, one patient, both IDs present, referral system to telemedicine system, validating green in CI. Nothing else. This proved every layer of the design in a single path — all seven steps are done.

  1. Constants module — identifier system URIs, canonical URLs, binding codes
  2. Registry table and GET /registry/v1/hcpn, seeded with two rows
  3. Intake endpoint with schema validation and idempotency
  4. Builders: Patient, Organization, Practitioner, PractitionerRole, ServiceRequest, Task, Encounter, Condition, Observation, Provenance
  5. Bundle assembler — transaction, urn:uuid references, conditional writes
  6. Two golden fixtures through the validator, wired into the existing CI gate
  7. Delivery to the telemedicine system, with patient resolution at the receiver

Two things came out differently than planned, both for the same reason. The conflicting-hit branch was built after all — leaving it out would have meant silently merging charts, which is worse than the work of implementing the refusal. Retries were not built, and will not be. A queue has to store the payload somewhere, and the payload is a patient's referral; since the sending system already owns the native record, it is the right place for it to wait. A failed submission is reported back and the sender resubmits the same message_id, which re-attempts rather than duplicating.

Still excluded: authentication, attachments as DocumentReference, and anything involving an external HCPN.

11 · Record

What this session changed

Two positions were reversed during review
A gateway-side patient match table was proposed, then dropped. It would have required storing names and birth dates, which contradicts the no-storage constraint, and it could not have worked on first contact anyway — a table that learns from traffic has nothing to match against the first time it sees a patient. Requiring both IDs at triage removes the need for it entirely. Questions about the MPI's availability were withdrawn. With identity settled at capture, the MPI is no longer on the request path by default, so its uptime is not a blocking concern for v1.
